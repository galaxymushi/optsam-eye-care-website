What Optsam does, what to expect, and how to tell when a problem should not wait. The services below are limited to those Optsam describes in its own materials.

Primary eye care

Optsam describes its role as examining eyesight, dispensing and prescribing corrective lenses, identifying and managing eye conditions, and referring patients to specialists.

The practice also runs an optical workshop for making and fitting spectacles. Availability of specific tests, lenses and frames can change; please confirm when you enquire.

01

Eye examinations

An eye examination is more than reading letters on a chart. It is a chance to understand how your eyes are working, how well you see at different distances, and whether anything needs attention.

Eyesight examination is the core of Optsam’s primary eye care. The practice describes assessing vision, prescribing corrective lenses, identifying eye conditions and referring patients to an ophthalmologist when specialist care is needed.

The exact tests and equipment used at your visit may vary. Ask when you arrange your appointment, and mention any changes in your vision or any eye symptoms you have noticed.

02

Prescription spectacles

A prescription is only half the work. The frame has to fit your face, the lenses have to suit how you use your eyes, and the finished pair has to be comfortable enough to wear.

Optsam prescribes and dispenses corrective lenses. Its optical workshop makes and fits spectacles, so the prescription, lenses and frame are considered together rather than in separate visits.

If you already wear spectacles, bring them with you, along with any previous prescription you have. Ask about the lens and frame options currently available before you choose.

03

Optical workshop

The workshop is where spectacles are made and lenses are fitted to frames. It is the practical side of the practice — and part of why Optsam calls itself an eye care and optical workshop store.

Optsam’s own materials describe a workshop for making all kinds of spectacles. Frame and lens availability changes, and the practice does not publish an online catalogue or price list.

Ask about current options, expected turnaround and what can be done with an existing pair of spectacles when you enquire.

Ask about the workshop

04

Contact lenses

Contact lenses are a different way of correcting vision — and they need a proper fitting, not just a prescription.

Optsam lists contact-lens fitting and supply among its services, including cosmetic and therapeutic lenses. Ask about the lens types currently available and whether contact lenses are suitable for your eyes.

If you already wear contact lenses, bring your current lenses and cleaning solution details to your appointment.

05

Referral & eye health

Primary eye care has limits. Knowing them is part of good care.

When a condition needs ophthalmology, Optsam refers patients to an eye specialist. A referral is not a failure of the examination; it is the right next step when a problem needs a different kind of expertise.

If you are unsure whether your problem is urgent, call and ask. If it is sudden or severe, do not wait for an appointment — go to the nearest hospital or emergency service.

06

Eye-health guidance

Part of Optsam’s work has always been explanation: helping people understand what is happening with their eyes and when to seek care.

That has included community screening, school visits, training for other health workers, and public education in English and Kiswahili. The aim is not to turn a consultation into a lecture, but to leave with a clearer understanding of your own sight.

Bring your questions. Ask what was found, what it means, and what should happen next.

When not to wait

Some symptoms need urgent care.

Go to the nearest hospital or emergency service immediately if you have:

  • a sudden loss or sudden change in vision;
  • severe eye pain, or a painful red eye;
  • a serious eye injury or a chemical splash;
  • new flashes of light or a sudden increase in floaters;
  • double vision that has just started.

This website provides general information, not medical advice or a diagnosis. Optsam does not advertise an emergency service.

Practical questions

A few things
worth asking.

If your question is not here, call or email. It is better to confirm than to travel on an assumption.

Do I need an appointment?

Optsam’s service flyer advertises appointment scheduling. Call to arrange a time — current availability is not published online.

How much does an eye examination or pair of spectacles cost?

Prices are not published on Optsam’s website or flyers. Please ask when you call or email, and confirm the cost before you commit to anything.

Can Optsam make a new pair from my existing prescription?

The workshop makes and fits spectacles. Ask about the current options and what information is needed when you enquire.

Can children be seen?

Optsam’s community work has included school eye screening. Call to ask about appointments for children and anything you should bring.

What if I need specialist care?

Optsam refers patients to an ophthalmologist when specialist care is needed. If your symptoms are sudden or severe, go directly to a hospital rather than waiting for an appointment.
