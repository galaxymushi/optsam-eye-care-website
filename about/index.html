About Optsam

01 / 06

A practice built around sight.

Optsam is the practice identity of J.M. Samwel, a Tanzanian optometrist. Its name joins a profession and a person; its story joins primary eye care with a long record of community work.

The name, the idea

Two things,
held together.

“Opt” for Optometrist. “Sam” for Samwel. Optsam began in 2017 as an idea for eye-health outreach, awareness and community service.

In 2019 that idea took on a business identity: Optsam Eye Care and Optical Workshop Store. It became the platform through which J.M. Samwel could examine eyesight, prescribe and dispense corrective lenses, and refer people to specialist care when they needed it.

The workshop was not an afterthought. Making and fitting spectacles is where clinical understanding meets daily life — the point at which a prescription becomes something a person can actually wear.

“What started as an idea to serve communities gradually became a professional journey through private practice, community service, consultancy, public education and, ultimately, public service.”

J.M. Samwel — published professional biography, 2026

02 · The founder

Samwel John Mushi

Professionally known as J.M. Samwel

A Tanzanian optometrist who trained at the School of Optometry, KCMC, Moshi, and completed field work at CCBRT in Dar es Salaam in 2017.

His professional path has moved between direct patient care, community screening, consultancy inside established clinics, training other health workers and public education. Optsam is the thread that connects them.

03 · A working history

The journey,
year by year.

A condensed record drawn from Optsam’s own professional biography and dated posts. It is a history of work, not a list of claims.

  1. 2017

    Training, field work — and an idea

    Graduates from the School of Optometry at Kilimanjaro Christian Medical Centre (KCMC), Moshi, after field work at CCBRT in Dar es Salaam. The name Optsam is formed from “Opt” and “Sam”.

  2. 2018

    Broadening the view

    Works with Diamond Optical Ltd. and takes part in ITV’s “Meza Huru” programme to speak to the public about glaucoma.

  3. 2019

    A business identity

    Establishes Optsam Eye Care and Optical Workshop Store as the identity through which private optometry services and consultancy are provided.

  4. 2020

    Into the community

    Eye-health activity with TAG Ukonga Church, retired prison officers in Ukonga and Rotary events. An Integrated People-centred Eye Care screening session at Ukonga is documented.

  5. 2021

    Consultancy and training

    Works with Tanzanites Lions Club, Aga Khan Polyclinic Tabata and Sokeny Polyclinic Mabibo. Facilitates Snellen-chart training for doctors and nurses with Tindwa Medical and Health Services.

  6. 2022

    World Sight Day

    Provides a free comprehensive eye screening for teachers and students at Migombani Primary School, Tabata Segerea.

  7. 2024

    Education in public

    Publishes the documentary script “Kuona ni kuamini / Seeing is believing” and documents a charitable mobile eye clinic at Kilamba Primary School, Mbagala Charambe.

  8. 2025–26

    A new chapter

    Continues eye-health education through TBC’s “Aridhio” and begins a transition into public-sector optometry, including service at Mchemo District Hospital, Mtwara.

Source: Optsam professional biography, 11 September 2026 and dated Optsam posts. Some dates refer to the year an activity was documented; they are not presented as a complete employment history.

A note on Optsam today

The chapter
has changed.

In September 2026, J.M. Samwel published an official statement explaining that he has transitioned from private practice into public service.

He describes Ask Optsam as a personal, non-commercial eye-health education initiative — separate from his government role, and not a substitute for clinical assessment or care. The practice and workshop history described on this site belongs to Optsam’s earlier private-practice chapter.

Current service availability is not published online. Please call or email before travelling, and seek appropriate clinical care if you need urgent attention.
